1. How to Use

Enter your portfolio holdings (ticker symbols) and their weights (must sum to 100%). Click Analyze Portfolio to run the analysis. The tool will:

1. Fetch 2 years of daily price data from Yahoo Finance
2. Compute portfolio math (correlations, volatility, diversification metrics)
3. Map each holding to a Damodaran industry classification
4. Score each holding across 10 risk factors
5. Detect hidden risk overlaps
6. Generate a plain-English risk narrative

You can then download a detailed Excel report (6 sheets with formulas) or a PDF report.

Supported Tickers

Any ticker available on Yahoo Finance works — US large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap, international ADRs, and ETFs (GLD, SPY, etc.). The industry mapping covers 94 Damodaran industries. Tickers without price data are excluded with a warning.

2. Diversification Metrics

HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index)
HHI = Σ wi²
Measures weight concentration. A portfolio with equal weights across N holdings has HHI = 1/N. A single-stock portfolio has HHI = 1.0.
Range: [1/N, 1.0] — Lower is more evenly distributed. Below 0.15 = well distributed, 0.15-0.25 = unconcentrated, above 0.25 = concentrated.
Neff (Effective Number of Bets)
Neff = (Σ λi)² / Σ λi²
Uses eigenvalue decomposition of the correlation matrix to measure how many truly independent risk sources exist. A portfolio of 10 highly correlated tech stocks might only have Neff of 2-3, meaning it behaves like 2-3 independent bets despite holding 10 names.
Range: [1, N] — Higher = more independent risk sources. The closer to N, the better diversified.
Diversification Ratio (DR)
DR = (Σ wi σi) / σportfolio
Ratio of the weighted sum of individual volatilities to the actual portfolio volatility. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this is always ≥ 1.0. The gap above 1.0 represents risk reduction from diversification.
Range: [1.0, ∞) — DR = 1.0 means zero diversification benefit (all perfectly correlated). DR of 1.5 means you've reduced portfolio risk to 67% of what it would be without diversification.
Portfolio Volatility (Annualized)
σp = √(w' · Cov · w)
Annualized standard deviation of portfolio returns, computed from the 252-day annualized covariance matrix. Approximately 68% of annual returns will fall within ± this value of the mean.
Typical range: 10-40% for equity portfolios.
Marginal Risk Contributions (MRC)
MRCi = wi × (Cov · w)i / σp
Each holding's contribution to total portfolio risk via Euler decomposition. MRCs always sum exactly to portfolio volatility. This shows which holdings are actually driving your portfolio's risk — a 10% position in a volatile, correlated stock can contribute more risk than a 30% position in a stable, uncorrelated one.
Sum of all MRCs = portfolio volatility. Available in the Excel report (Sheet 2).
Distance to Diversification
Distance = 1 - (Neff - 1) / (target - 1)
How far the portfolio is from a well-diversified benchmark (target = min(30, N)). A score of 0 means fully diversified; 1.0 means single-stock concentrated.
Range: [0, 1] — Lower is better. Below 0.3 = close to diversified, 0.3-0.6 = room to improve, above 0.6 = far from diversified.

3. The 10 Risk Factors

Each holding is scored 1-10 on these factors based on its Damodaran industry classification. Portfolio scores are weighted averages by allocation.

1
Interest Rate Risk
Sensitivity to interest rate changes. Long-duration assets like REITs, utilities, and life insurance score 7-9. Growth sectors with near-term cash flows score 2-4.
High: REITs (9), Banks (9), Utilities (8) — Low: Software (3), Semiconductors (3)
2
China Revenue Risk
Estimated revenue exposure to China by sector. Companies with significant China sales face tariff, trade war, and market access risks.
High: Semiconductors (8), Electronics (8), Auto (7) — Low: Banks (2-4), Utilities (1), Healthcare (3)
3
Geopolitical/Country Risk
Exposure to emerging/frontier market instability and geopolitical tensions. Based on Damodaran country equity risk premiums weighted by revenue geography.
High: Mining (7), Oil/Gas (6), Semiconductors (6) — Low: Domestic services (2-3)
4
Currency Risk
Percentage of revenue earned in foreign currencies. Exchange rate volatility can significantly swing reported earnings for companies with global operations.
High: Software Internet (7), Pharma (7), Semiconductors (7) — Low: Banks Regional (3), Utilities (2)
5
Regulatory/Policy Risk
Vulnerability to government regulation, antitrust enforcement, or policy shifts. Updated to reflect current regulatory environment including Big Tech antitrust actions.
High: Banks (9), Big Tech/Internet (7), Healthcare (8) — Low: Machinery (3), Retail (3)
6
Leverage/Credit Risk
Quantitatively derived from Damodaran D/E ratios. Higher leverage means more sensitivity to credit conditions, interest expense, and refinancing risk.
Scale: D/E ≤5% → 1, ≤25% → 3, ≤50% → 5, ≤100% → 7, >250% → 10
7
Earnings Cyclicality
Quantitatively derived from Damodaran standard deviation of operating income. Measures how much earnings swing with economic cycles.
Scale: StdDev ≤10% → 1, ≤28% → 4, ≤52% → 6, ≤100% → 8, >185% → 10
8
Sector Concentration
Risk from GICS sector overlap. Multiple holdings in the same sector amplify sector-specific shocks. A portfolio of all tech stocks has high sector concentration risk even if the individual companies differ.
High: Banks (8), Semiconductors (8), Software (6) — Low: Diversified conglomerates (2)
9
Supply Chain Concentration
Dependency on concentrated supply chains, particularly Taiwan/TSMC for semiconductor manufacturing. A single point of failure in the supply chain can halt production across the industry.
High: Semiconductors (10), Computers (8), Electronics (7) — Low: Banks (1), Insurance (1), Software (4)
10
ESG/Energy Transition Risk
Exposure to fossil fuel dependency, carbon regulation, and stranded asset risk from the energy transition. Companies in carbon-intensive industries face regulatory and market headwinds.
High: Oil/Gas (10), Coal (10), Steel (7) — Low: Software (2), Healthcare (3)

5. Risk Overlaps

An overlap is flagged when 2 or more holdings both score 6 or higher on the same risk factor. This reveals hidden concentration: even a "diversified" portfolio can have correlated risk if multiple holdings share the same vulnerability.

Severity Levels

SeverityCriteriaWhat It Means
CRITICALCombined weight > 60% AND avg score ≥ 8Majority of portfolio exposed to a single extreme risk — a single event could cause severe drawdown
HIGHCombined weight > 40% OR avg score ≥ 8Significant concentration in one risk dimension
MEDIUMCombined weight > 25%Notable overlap worth monitoring
LOW2+ holdings above threshold, lower weightMinor overlap, limited portfolio impact

6. Correlation Matrix

Shows pairwise return correlations over the 2-year lookback period. This measures how similarly two stocks' returns move on a daily basis.

Color coding: Red = high positive correlation (stocks move together), White = no correlation (independent), Green = negative correlation (stocks move opposite).

Key insight: Diversification benefit comes from low correlations. If all your holdings are red (correlated), adding more stocks won't meaningfully reduce risk. Look for holdings that are white or green relative to your existing positions.

The correlation matrix is also used to compute Neff via eigenvalue decomposition, and to build the covariance matrix for MRC calculations.

7. Data Sources

Market Data
Yahoo Finance (yfinance) — daily closing prices, sector/industry classification, and company fundamentals. 2-year lookback period by default. Cached locally for 24 hours.
Industry Betas & D/E Ratios
Damodaran Online (pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar) — levered/unlevered betas, D/E ratios, effective tax rates, and operating income volatility for 94 industry groups. Data as of January 2026. Cached for 7 days, with fallback to bundled data if network fails.
Country Equity Risk Premiums
Damodaran ctryprem dataset — 178 countries with Moody's ratings, CDS spreads, and equity risk premiums. Used for geopolitical/country risk scoring.
Risk Factor Scores
Factors 6 (Leverage) and 7 (Cyclicality) are quantitatively derived from Damodaran data using defined breakpoint tables. Other factors use domain-knowledge-based assignments at the industry level, benchmarked against S&P 500 sector weights.

Limitations

Risk scores are assigned at the industry level, not the individual company level. A small-cap software company gets the same scores as Microsoft. Correlation and volatility data is backward-looking and may not predict future relationships. Damodaran data is updated approximately annually.
